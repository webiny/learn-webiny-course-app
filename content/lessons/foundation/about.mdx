## About this course

This is a course for developers, and it's designed to provide a comprehensive understanding of Webiny, its apps, architecture, and extensibility model, and how to effectively utilize its features for content management, website building, and application development. By the end of this course, you will have a solid foundation in Webiny and will be equipped to build, customize, and manage content-driven applications and websites.

We structured the course into several chapters, each focusing on different aspects of Webiny. You will learn about its core components, how to set up and configure a Webiny project, how to customize its behavior, and general best practices. All the learning will be done through practical examples and hands-on exercises, so you can apply your knowledge in real-world scenarios.

As Webiny continues to evolve into an AI-programmable CMS, this course will also help you understand the foundations that make that possible: open architecture, strong typing, extensibility, and a developer-first approach to building content platforms. These fundamentals matter whether you're writing code directly, working with scaffolds, or using AI tools and agents to accelerate development. Once you've worked through them, the final chapter — *Building with AI Agents and Webiny MCP Server* — turns those foundations into the actual day-to-day workflow.

We encourage you to follow along with the practical examples and exercises provided throughout the course to reinforce your learning. In case you have any questions or need further clarification, feel free to reach out to the Webiny community and support channels. All the details are in the sidebar on the left.

## Why Webiny?

Webiny was created to address the growing need for a flexible, scalable, and developer-friendly content platform that can handle complex content management requirements. We're talking projects with large amounts of content, multiple content types, complex editorial workflows, and deep customization needs.

From day one, Webiny was built for developers who need more than a fixed CMS product. It was built as a platform they can shape — through code, APIs, extensions, lifecycle hooks, and custom application logic — while still giving content teams a polished editorial experience.

That developer-first foundation is also what makes Webiny especially relevant in the AI era. While many platforms focus on using AI to generate content, Webiny is designed so developers can use AI to help generate capabilities inside the platform itself — whether that's new workflows, integrations, UI extensions, or custom content experiences.

Webiny aims to fill this gap by providing a modern, scalable architecture that allows developers to build and manage a content platform with ease. Developers can leverage familiar technologies like React, GraphQL, and TypeScript, while content editors benefit from a user-friendly interface for managing content.

In the real world, Webiny handles projects with 100's of millions of records, peta-bytes of assets, and thousands of content editors — all while providing a highly reliable, secure, and cost-effective solution.

But Webiny is not for everyone. In the next sections, we'll explore when to use Webiny and when it might not be the best fit for your project.

<IconTitle icon="thumb_up" title="When to use Webiny?" heading="h3" />

Webiny is an excellent choice for projects that require:

- **Data privacy and control**: Webiny is self-hosted so you have full control over your data and infrastructure, allowing you to comply with data privacy regulations and maintain ownership of your content.
- **Scalability**: Webiny's architecture allows applications to scale automatically based on demand, making it suitable for projects with fluctuating traffic or very large and sudden spikes.
- **Customization**: Webiny's built-in framework enables developers to customize and extend every part of the platform to meet specific project requirements, both the UI and the API side.
- **Operational efficiency**: Webiny's features, such as content modeling, page building, content approval workflows, and asset management, streamline content management processes and improve editorial workflows, especially within large and distributed teams.
- **AI-assisted development**: Webiny ships with a built-in MCP server and a curated library of skills that teach AI coding agents how to extend the platform correctly — so teams can use AI to accelerate custom features, integrations, workflows, and editorial experiences without producing one-off code that fights the platform.
- **Security**: Webiny is architected with security in mind, providing features such as role-based access control, folder level permissions, field permissions, SOC2 compliance, SSO integration and secure content delivery.
- **Multi-tenant architecture**: Webiny supports multi-tenant setups, making it ideal for organizations managing multiple project from a single instance or for embedding inside existing SaaS products to enrich existing offerings with content management capabilities.

<IconTitle icon="thumb_down" title="When not to use Webiny?" heading="h3" />

These are just some of the examples where Webiny might not be the best fit:

- **Simple websites or applications**: For small-scale projects with minimal content management needs, a lightweight CMS or static site generator may be more appropriate.
- **Limited technical expertise**: Webiny requires a certain level of technical knowledge to set up and manage. If your team lacks the necessary skills (React, TypeScript, Node, GraphQL), you may need to invest in training, hire additional resources or work with a Webiny partner.
- **Specific technology stacks**: If your project relies heavily on technologies that are not compatible with Webiny's architecture or framework, you may need to consider alternative solutions.
- **On-premises hosting requirements**: Webiny is designed for cloud-based deployments, primarily on AWS. If your project requires on-premises hosting due to regulatory or organizational policies, Webiny may not be suitable.
- **Teams looking for a no-code, closed-box SaaS tool**: If your priority is a plug-and-play CMS with minimal developer involvement and little need for customization, Webiny is probably more platform power than you need.

## Webiny licenses

Webiny comes in three editions: Community, Business and Enterprise. All three are built on the same open-source code base; Business and Enterprise add features on top of it.

**The Community edition** is open source and free to use under the MIT License (<ExternalLink href="https://github.com/webiny/webiny-js">GitHub</ExternalLink>). It includes the core of the platform: the Headless CMS, Website Builder and File Manager. It suits individual developers and teams that need a content platform they can customize and extend.

**The Business edition** is a paid plan that adds role-based access control, multi-tenancy and publishing workflows. It has a free tier, so you can try these features before you pay.

**The Enterprise edition** adds team management, SSO, audit logs and priority support, on an annual subscription. It is meant for large organizations with strict security and compliance requirements.

<Callout type="info" title="Choosing the right edition">

</Callout>
